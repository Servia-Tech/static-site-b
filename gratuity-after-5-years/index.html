Gratuity After 5 Years: the 21 vs 30 Day Bands

Passing five years raises your rate — but only for the years that follow. Here is the mistake almost everyone makes.

Last reviewed: 26 September 2026 · General information, not legal advice

The misconception

Federal Decree-Law 33/2021 sets two rates: 21 days of basic pay per year for the first five years, and 30 days per year thereafter. A very common assumption is that once you cross five years, the whole period is recalculated at 30 days.

It is not. The bands are cumulative, not retroactive. Your first five years stay at 21 days permanently; only the sixth year onward earns 30.

ServiceCorrect calculationCommon wrong answer
6 years(5 × 21) + (1 × 30) = 135 days6 × 30 = 180 days
10 years(5 × 21) + (5 × 30) = 255 days10 × 30 = 300 days
15 years(5 × 21) + (10 × 30) = 405 days15 × 30 = 450 days

On a basic salary of AED 6,000, the ten-year difference between the correct and the wrong figure is AED 9,000 — enough to be worth understanding before you sign a settlement.

Worked example: exactly 5 years vs 5 years and 1 month

Basic salary AED 9,000, so the daily wage is 9,000 ÷ 30 = AED 300.

At exactly 5 years

5 × 21 × 300 = AED 31,500

At 5 years and 1 month

  • First 5 years: 5 × 21 × 300 = AED 31,500
  • Extra month: (1 ÷ 12) × 30 × 300 = AED 750
  • Total: AED 32,250

So the sixth year is worth about 43% more per month than the fifth. There is no cliff or bonus at the five-year mark — just a higher rate from that point forward.

The two-year cap, and when it actually applies

Total gratuity cannot exceed two years' wages. People worry about this far more than they need to. Working through it:

  • Applied on basic pay (the usual reading of "two years' wage" in Article 51(6)), two years = 24 months = 720 days of basic pay
  • The first five years produce 105 days
  • Each further year produces 30 days
  • 720 − 105 = 615 days, ÷ 30 = 20.5 further years

The cap therefore only bites at roughly 25.5 years of continuous service with one employer. For the overwhelming majority of employees it is irrelevant.

Things that do and do not affect the bands

FactorEffect on your gratuity
Resigning rather than being terminatedNone — since February 2022
A salary increase during serviceCalculated on your last basic salary, so a rise raises the whole figure
Unpaid leaveDoes not count as service, so it reduces the total
Paid annual leaveCounts normally
Allowances (housing, transport)Excluded — basic salary only
Probation periodCounts towards service once you pass one year overall

The salary point is worth dwelling on. Because gratuity uses your final basic salary rather than an average, a promotion shortly before leaving increases the value of every prior year. This is the single largest lever on the final number, and it is the opposite of how funded schemes such as DIFC's DEWS work.

Frequently asked questions

Does gratuity double after 5 years in the UAE?

No. The rate rises from 21 to 30 days per year, but only for years after the fifth. Your first five years remain at 21 days each and are not recalculated.

How much gratuity do I get after 10 years in the UAE?

255 days of basic pay: five years at 21 days (105 days) plus five years at 30 days (150 days). On a basic salary of AED 6,000, that is roughly AED 51,000.

Is UAE gratuity capped?

Yes, at two years' total wages. In practice that ceiling is only reached after roughly 25 years of continuous service with the same employer, so it rarely applies.

Is gratuity based on my first or last salary?

Your last basic salary. A pay rise late in your service therefore increases the value of all your earlier years, which makes final-salary gratuity notably generous for long-serving employees who have been promoted.

Does my probation period count towards gratuity?

Yes, provided you go on to complete at least one year of continuous service overall. Probation is part of your service, not separate from it.

Do I get more gratuity if I am made redundant rather than resigning?

Not in terms of gratuity — the calculation is identical either way. Termination may give rise to separate claims, such as compensation for arbitrary dismissal, but the gratuity figure itself does not change.
