UAE Unemployment Insurance (ILOE) Explained

A small monthly premium that pays 60% of your basic salary for up to three months if you lose your job involuntarily.

Last reviewed: 26 September 2026 · General information, not legal advice

What ILOE is

The Involuntary Loss of Employment scheme, introduced by Federal Decree-Law No. 13 of 2022, is a mandatory unemployment insurance for most employees in the mainland UAE and many free zones. You pay a small monthly premium; if you subsequently lose your job through no fault of your own, it pays a cash benefit while you look for work.

It is genuinely insurance, not savings. If you never claim, you do not get the premiums back — which is why the cost is so low.

The two categories

Category ACategory B
WhoBasic salary AED 16,000 or lessBasic salary above AED 16,000
PremiumAED 5 per monthAED 10 per month

Premiums may be paid monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually, and there is usually a small VAT charge on top. Subscription is the employee's responsibility, not the employer's — this is the single most common reason people find they are not covered when they need to claim.

What the benefit actually pays

  • 60% of your average basic salary over the six months before you lost your job
  • Capped at your category maximum — AED 10,000 or AED 20,000 a month
  • Paid for a maximum of three months per claim
  • Subject to a lifetime aggregate cap on total months claimed across your career

Note it is 60% of basic, not gross. On a gross salary of AED 20,000 with a basic of AED 12,000, the benefit is 60% of 12,000 = AED 7,200 a month, not 60% of 20,000.

Who can claim, and who cannot

You can claim if

  • You have subscribed for at least 12 consecutive months
  • Your job loss was involuntary — redundancy, termination, non-renewal
  • You are still in the UAE and legally able to work
  • You file within the required window after your job ends — this is short, so do not delay

You cannot claim if

  • You resigned
  • You were dismissed for disciplinary reasons or gross misconduct
  • You have not completed the 12-month subscription period
  • You have left the country or are otherwise unavailable for work
  • Your premiums were unpaid — a lapsed subscription generally means no cover

The resignation exclusion is fundamental. ILOE covers involuntary loss of employment, which is quite different from gratuity, where resignation makes no difference at all.

How ILOE relates to your final settlement

They are entirely separate, and one does not reduce the other:

Receiving gratuity does not prevent an ILOE claim, and claiming ILOE does not reduce your gratuity. If you are made redundant, you may well be entitled to both.

Frequently asked questions

What is ILOE in the UAE?

The Involuntary Loss of Employment scheme, a mandatory unemployment insurance introduced by Federal Decree-Law 13 of 2022. Employees pay a small monthly premium and receive a cash benefit if they lose their job involuntarily.

How much does ILOE pay?

60% of your average basic salary over the six months before job loss, capped at AED 10,000 a month for Category A or AED 20,000 for Category B, for a maximum of three months per claim.

How much does ILOE cost?

AED 5 a month if your basic salary is AED 16,000 or less, or AED 10 a month if it is above that, plus a small VAT charge. Premiums can be paid monthly or in advance.

Can I claim ILOE if I resign?

No. ILOE covers involuntary loss of employment only. Resignation and dismissal for disciplinary reasons both disqualify you from claiming.

Who is responsible for subscribing to ILOE?

The employee, not the employer. This surprises many people, and an unpaid or lapsed subscription generally means no cover when you need it.

Does claiming ILOE reduce my gratuity?

No. They are entirely separate — gratuity is paid by your employer based on service, while ILOE is paid from an insurance pool. If you are made redundant you may be entitled to both.
