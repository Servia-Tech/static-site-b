Unpaid Salary or Gratuity: How to Complain to MOHRE

If your employer has not paid, there is a free, established process — and you do not need a lawyer to start it.

Last reviewed: 26 September 2026 · General information, not legal advice

First, check the deadline has actually passed

Under Federal Decree-Law 33/2021, your employer must pay your full final settlement within 14 days of the contract end date. Before escalating, confirm:

  • Your last working day, and whether 14 days have genuinely elapsed
  • Whether a partial payment has been made — this changes what you are claiming
  • Whether a written explanation for a deduction has been given

A great many disputes are actually calculation disagreements rather than refusals to pay — most often gratuity computed on the wrong salary base, or leave encashment omitted. Work out what you believe you are owed first, so your complaint states a specific figure.

Step 1 — Raise it in writing with your employer

Send a short, factual email to HR stating what is unpaid, the amount, and the statutory 14-day deadline. Keep it unemotional and specific. This matters for two reasons: it often resolves the issue immediately, and if it does not, it becomes evidence that you attempted to settle the matter directly.

Keep every reply. Do not rely on verbal assurances or WhatsApp voice notes.

Step 2 — File a labour complaint

If your employer is mainland, the complaint goes to the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation. There are several routes:

  • The MOHRE app or the MOHRE website
  • The MOHRE call centre on 600590000
  • A Tas'heel service centre in person

Filing is free. You do not need a lawyer at this stage, and you should be wary of anyone charging a large fee simply to submit the form for you.

If your employer is in a free zone, the complaint usually goes to the free zone authority that registered your contract instead. If you are in DIFC or ADGM, those have their own employment tribunals and processes entirely. Which regime applies to you →

Step 3 — Conciliation

MOHRE attempts to settle the dispute between the parties first. Both sides are called, the claim is reviewed, and a settlement is proposed. Many cases end here, because the process is quick and employers generally prefer not to escalate.

If conciliation does not resolve it, MOHRE decides the dispute itself where the claim is AED 50,000 or less (or where the employer has breached an earlier amicable settlement); either party can appeal that decision to the Court of Appeal within 15 working days. Larger claims are referred to the court (Article 54 of Federal Decree-Law 33/2021, as amended by Federal Decree-Law 9/2024).

Evidence to gather before you file

The strength of your case depends almost entirely on documentation. Collect:

  • Your employment contract — this establishes your basic salary, which drives the gratuity and leave calculations
  • Payslips and bank statements showing what was actually paid
  • WPS records if you were paid through the Wage Protection System — MOHRE can see these directly and they are strong evidence
  • Your resignation or termination letter, establishing the end date and who ended the contract
  • Leave records showing days accrued and taken
  • Any written exchange about the unpaid amount

The contract matters most. Because gratuity and leave encashment are both calculated on basic salary, a contract that does not clearly separate basic from allowances is the root of a large share of disputes.

Practical points worth knowing

  • Do not sign a settlement you disagree with. A signed full-and-final receipt makes a later claim substantially harder. If pressured, say you need time to review it.
  • Your visa and your money are separate matters. An employer cannot lawfully make payment conditional on you waiving claims, and visa cancellation is a distinct process.
  • Time limits apply. Claims under the labour law are not heard after two years from the end of the employment relationship (Article 54(9), as amended in 2024). Do not let it drift.
  • Leaving the country does not extinguish your claim, but it makes pursuing it considerably harder in practice. Start the process before you go if you can.

Frequently asked questions

What do I do if my employer will not pay my gratuity?

Raise it in writing with HR first, stating the amount and the 14-day statutory deadline. If that fails, file a free labour complaint with MOHRE via the app, the call centre on 600590000 or a Tas'heel centre. Free zone employees complain to their free zone authority instead.

How long does my employer have to pay my final settlement?

14 days from the contract end date under Federal Decree-Law 33/2021. After that you can escalate.

Does it cost money to file a labour complaint in the UAE?

Filing with MOHRE is free and you do not need a lawyer to start the process. Be cautious of intermediaries charging significant fees simply to submit the form.

Should I sign the final settlement if I think it is wrong?

No. Signing a full-and-final receipt makes a later claim significantly harder. You are entitled to take time to review the calculation before signing.

Can I still claim after I leave the UAE?

Your claim does not disappear, but pursuing it from abroad is considerably harder in practice. If there is a dispute, it is much better to begin the process before you depart.

What evidence do I need?

Your employment contract above all, since it establishes your basic salary. Also payslips, bank statements, WPS records, your resignation or termination letter, leave records and any written exchange about the unpaid amount.
